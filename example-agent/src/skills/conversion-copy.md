# Conversion copywriting rules

These are the rules the copywriter follows. They're opinionated on purpose.
When in doubt, prefer specificity over cleverness.

## Headline (`<h1>`)

- **Lead with the outcome, not the product.** "Get paid 3 days sooner" beats "Modern invoicing software."
- **One promise.** A headline that's two promises is a headline that converts on neither.
- **6–12 words** is the sweet spot. Anything longer is a subhead.
- **Avoid:** "Unlock", "Revolutionize", "Empower", "AI-powered" (for the AI's
  benefit too — these signal Marketing Voice that's been shown to underperform).

Good:
> Plant-based dinners that don't ask for thirty minutes you don't have.

Bad:
> Unlock the power of plant-based eating with revolutionary meal kits.

## Subhead

- **1 sentence**, 12-24 words.
- **Adds the proof** the headline assumed. Numbers, time-savings, comparisons.
- **Names the audience** when the headline didn't ("for parents", "for solo accountants").

## Value props (body)

- Two paragraphs, each focused on one specific benefit.
- Lead each paragraph with the benefit, not the feature.
  - Bad: "Our app uses end-to-end encryption."
  - Good: "Your client data never leaves your laptop, even when you're synced across devices."
- Concrete numbers beat adjectives. "15 minutes" beats "fast". "30+ recipes" beats "lots of variety".

## CTA

- **Verb + specific outcome.** "Try a box" beats "Get Started". "See your savings" beats "Learn more".
- 2-4 words.
- **One primary CTA** on the page. A secondary "see how it works" is fine. Three or more is none.

## Voice

Default to: **warm, confident, specific.** No jargon. No emoji in headlines.
If the user named a brand (e.g. "Stripe-style"), match its known voice:
- Stripe: technical, calm, understatement.
- Linear: precise, modern, no-bullshit.
- Anthropic: editorial, honest, careful.

Otherwise: warm and specific.
